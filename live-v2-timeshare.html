For many owners, the real trap begins when they finally try to leave.

On March 29, 2024, the FTC published a consumer alert aimed directly at timeshare owners who are told someone already has a buyer for their property. The warning is blunt: if the caller wants an upfront fee to finalize the sale, stop. According to the FTC, that is one of the clearest signs the owner is dealing with a resale scam rather than a legitimate transaction.

That matters because many owners reach this point only after the numbers stop making sense. Annual dues continue, surprise assessments appear, and the resale market is so weak that the promise of a fast buyer can sound like relief. In practice, that is often exactly what scammers exploit.

What the government has actually done, in plain English.

The shift in this space is not just rhetorical. On November 22, 2022, the Justice Department, the FTC, and the State of Wisconsin announced a civil enforcement action against 16 defendants for allegedly using deceptive sales practices to sell timeshare exit services to senior citizens. According to DOJ, the complaint said consumers were told they could not exit on their own, that heirs would be stuck with large fees, and that the defendants failed to notify consumers of their rights under federal and state law to cancel within three business days. DOJ also said the complaint alleged that mostly senior consumers paid more than $90 million for services that were not delivered.

By April 1, 2026, that enforcement effort had turned into a court ruling: DOJ said the Eastern District of Missouri granted summary judgment, found the operation harmed more than 11,000 consumers, permanently enjoined Christopher Lee Carroll from marketing timeshare exit services, ordered more than $95 million in consumer redress, and imposed more than $45 million in civil monetary penalties.
